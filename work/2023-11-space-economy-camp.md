## What it is

A camp run in November 2023 by ASU's Complexity Economics Lab with the 100 Years Starship Initiative and the Space Prize. Twenty places were offered to science fiction and fantasy authors through an open call, and the authors paid to attend. The format was one evening lecture and two full days of work sessions, three economics lectures and two economist keynotes, including Alberto Cottica's "Designer economies", which asks how much freedom we really have when imagining believable economic systems.

## What we did

Four groups, each with an economist and a facilitator, took one setting each (Earth orbit, the Moon, Mars and the asteroid belt) and wrote its future history, committing it to an artefact, usually a final presentation. Edgeryders then wrote a reflection on what the camp meant for the Sci-Fi Economics Seminar.

## How it turned out

The reflection is candid. The economists found the format mostly fun and engaging, and the writers said the concepts inspired them. The three economists, who had not met beforehand, gave lectures that formed an arc from values and incentives to Ostrom's commons and utopias as game theory. But writers struggled to work the concepts into their worlds, time pressure let inconsistencies slide, and they put more detail into governance, spiritual life and mental health care than into regulators and central banks. The lectures had also skipped development, money, banking and credit, production methods and ownership regimes.

The thread recommends a longer event, possibly five days, groups of about eight split evenly between creatives and social scientists, a fixed economics curriculum with handouts, and free attendance with the host providing the venue. Those lessons are visible in the design of the [[2024-05-science-fiction-economics-residency-in-messina|2024 Messina residency]].
