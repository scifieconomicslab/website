## What it is

Watchdog was co-founded in April 2019, two days after the Easter Sunday bombings, by Yudhanjaya Wijeratne with Bhanuka Harischandra, Nisal Periyapperuma, Ragulan Ketheeswaran and Safra Anver. The team built a fact-checking app in 36 hours and within three months had 25 to 30 volunteers and over 200,000 users.

## What we did

In January 2022, as the economy collapsed, it relaunched as an open-source intelligence collective, which Yudhanjaya called a junkyard version of Bellingcat. The Organized Crime and Corruption Reporting Project provided $340,000, which paid for a full-time team publishing in English, Sinhala and Tamil. By mid-2022 the team was twelve people. Its protest tracker geolocated and verified protest sites from public and crowdsourced data, and covered 597 protests and 49 conflicts, with 116 sites verified in its first week. It also built Project Elixir, an open-source database of crowdsourced medical shortages that matches requests with regulator-approved suppliers, which drew over 50 responses in two days in April 2022. A power-cut analysis linked blackouts to debt-strapped state utilities.

## How it turned out

Reuters used the protest data for nationwide mapping, and the Daily Mirror used it to challenge the prime minister's claim that protests reflected a minority view, noting that most were in Rajapaksa strongholds. Rest of World reports that Watchdog reached over a million people across its app, website and social media in April 2022, and that the charity Sarvodaya planned to roll Elixir out across all 25 districts.
