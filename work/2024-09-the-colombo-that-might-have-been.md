## What it is

A virtual Colombo built in Cities: Skylines by Watchdog in 2024. Topography, road network and land use follow the real city on an 8 by 8 metre grid, with land use drawn from official development plans centred on 2020. It holds 1,044,500 virtual citizens against a Colombo Municipal Council estimate of about 1,048,000, with about half living in the city and half commuting in along seven main arteries, as in reality. Citizens age, go to school, work and die, traffic follows Sri Lankan habits such as tuk-tuks and roadside parking, and public transport runs on real route data: three railway lines and six major bus routes. Against official figures the map's road layout, parks and water bodies match to over 99 percent, its length is 7,728 m against 7,780 m, and the vehicle ratio is calibrated to about 206 per 1,000 people.

## What we did

The team used the replica to test the first two phases of COMTRANS, the transport plan assembled by the Japan International Cooperation Agency. Phase one is a multimodal hub at Fort and Pettah linking rail, bus and a future monorail with the Colombo-Katunayake Expressway. Phase two widens Galle Road, Baseline Road, Kandy Road and Marine Drive to six lanes with bus lanes and sidewalks, on a 29 m cross-section with two 3.4 m bus lanes and four 3.2 m general lanes. Phases three to five, covering new major roads, bus rapid transit and the monorail, were left unbuilt and offered to readers as a challenge.

The work was led by LIRNEasia, a regional digital policy think tank, under the SCOPE programme co-funded by the European Union and the German Federal Foreign Office and implemented by GIZ with Sri Lanka's Ministry of Justice. Professor Amal Kumarage and Dr Amila Buddhika Jayasinghe supplied data and analysis of commuter flows, and the team also consulted experts at the University of Moratuwa. Data came from the Colombo Municipal Council, the Urban Development Authority and the Department of Census and Statistics, with Meta's population mapping data for the population. Code is released under the MIT licence on GitHub with a wiki, so anyone can run their own scenarios.

## What to take from it

The article puts the numbers beside the picture. COMTRANS projected about 5 million people in Greater Colombo by 2035, and its cost of 2.78 trillion rupees at 2014 prices comes to roughly 7 trillion rupees today by the authors' estimate, close to half of Sri Lanka's GDP. Widening roads displaces businesses and means years of negotiation and compensation. Enforced cycle lanes on Marine Drive would hurt shops that depend on roadside parking, so parking or road pricing has to be part of the plan. The authors conclude that the plan's 2020, 2025 and 2035 timelines are too optimistic and caution against promising monorails and bus lanes inside one election cycle.

## How it turned out

The results are qualitative: how plans affect neighbourhoods, traffic flow and businesses, shown on a map people can look at. The article reports no measured outcome or formal adoption. The project page says it was widely shared, including on the Cities: Skylines forums and Hacker News.
