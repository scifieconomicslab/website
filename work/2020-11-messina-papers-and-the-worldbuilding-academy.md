## What it is

Two linked pieces of work from autumn 2020. The first is a set of papers on the Messina Advanced Social District, a cluster of enterprises around Fondazione di Comunita di Messina (FdCM), published under CC BY 4.0 with support from EIT Climate-KIC. Alberto Cottica wrote the introduction and conducted the conversation, Nadia El-Imam wrote the premises, and FdCM's Gaetano Giunta and Giacomo Pinaffo explained the cluster in a webinar on 8 September 2020. The second is the Worldbuilding Academy, an open-source laboratory that brings science fiction writers and economists together, introduced in a webinar on 7 December 2020.

## What we did

The Lab studied the cluster as an action-research case, through a case study, the webinar and breakout discussions on Edgeryders. The context is hard: in 2018 Sicily had the highest unemployment rate in Italy, above 20 percent, and 22.5 percent of families were below the relative poverty line, with roughly a fifth of the economy informal or illegal.

The cluster's history, as the paper tells it, starts with a research centre in 1998, three linked organisations in 2000 (a research and culture foundation, a social cooperative and a financial fund against loan sharks) and FdCM itself in 2010, with initial capital of 5 million euros, about half from members and half from Fondazione con il Sud. Today it is about 120 enterprises employing over 400 people, around 100 of them disadvantaged.

The paper describes several projects. FdCM installed 2 MW of photovoltaic capacity through partners under Italy's feed-in tariffs. For 56 patients leaving a judicial psychiatric hospital, the State agreed to pay a lump sum equal to one year's spending per person, and the money funded solar plants and cooperative jobs. About 2,500 people live in slums dating from 1908, and around 200 families, about 700 people, entered a project offering public housing, a new eco-building or help to buy a home. After Heineken closed the Messina brewery, the laid-off workers formed a cooperative with a 6 million euro investment, which reached 5 million litres a year within three years and was profitable in its first.

The paper's conceptual claims are the part economists will want to argue with. Holding capital as stock rather than spending it as flow gives the foundation autonomy and ten-year plans. Relational goods get their value from the relationships they encode, like the art collection. Members change their own work to fit the whole, so it works as a system and not only a network. Cooperative governance, one member one vote, limits outside equity.

## How it turned out

The papers and webinar recording are public, and the 2020 paper describes FdCM as recognised by the OECD, UNOPS and WHO as one of the most interesting cases in welfare and local development experimentation. The foundation's own recognitions page, read in 2026, lists different bodies: REVES, the European network of cities and regions for the social economy, which called it one of the most interesting case studies in Europe; the OECD's LEED programme; the European federation of ethical and alternative banks, FEBEA; and the Global Alliance for Banking on Values, which chose it as the only Italian case among 23 worldwide. A 2025 interview on the Italian labour ministry's site adds that the foundation won the Premio Innovazione Sociale in 2025, has supported more than 260 social and green enterprises, and helped about 650 people move out of shantytowns into homes they chose, nearly half owner-occupied. Those later figures are larger than the 2020 ones above, and they are the foundation's own. The Worldbuilding Academy source is an announcement and reports no results. The cluster became the field visit on day one of the 2024 residency and the real-world anchor of [[2025-03-the-great-retrofit-world|The Great Retrofit]].
