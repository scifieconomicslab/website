## What it is

A fictional but carefully costed future for Messina, Sicily, written as if looking back from 2050. It is one group's output from the Science Fiction Economics Residency (6 to 9 May 2024) and it is published on Edgeryders as a set of linked pages: a start page, a timeline, a policy page and profiles of invented institutions. The group was eight people, listed on the start page by forum handle, and Ha-Joon Chang worked with them on the first two days.

The world's central idea is a new kind of company, a for-benefit firm that reports against a quintuple bottom line: surplus (not profit), people, planet, beauty and truth, meaning that it shares what it knows. Its main example is Priu, a cooperative of construction workers whose name is a Sicilian word for joy or satisfaction.

## What we did

The group wrote the world's history and then its policy. In the timeline, the Fondazione organises construction workers into Priu in 2027. In 2034 the city and the Foundation launch the Great Retrofit, a local moonshot to renovate 60,000 housing units, about 60 percent of the city's stock, within ten years, and the group estimates this cuts the city's CO2 emissions by about 15 percent. Rising raw material prices then push Priu towards circular supply chains.

The policy page is where the design shows. Owners get a tax credit against renovation invoices, and a credit can be sold only once: secondary trading and securitisation are banned, and ethical banks buy credits at a small discount. Residents pay the gap between the cost of the work and the credit. A liquidity fund backed by the national economic development ministry and ethical banks advances that gap to households short of cash and is repaid from energy savings. Low-income households receive grants. The oldest and most dilapidated buildings go first, because they gain the most per square metre and their occupants can least afford the work. To stop displacement, landlords of renovated rented homes cannot raise rents beyond inflation for seven years after the works. A community land trust holds donated land and leases it for up to 99 years at a nominal rent, with statutes that bar sales, and a School of Living Together trains new cohousing groups in governance and building management.

## What to take from it

Each mechanism answers a failure someone would raise at a council meeting: who pays up front, what happens to rents, and who is left out. The discussion on the start page cites Italy's 110 percent retrofit credit as a real-world scheme that went wrong, and one commenter points to Portugal's SAAL housing programme of the 1970s as a precedent. A mayor can disagree with any single rule, and that is the point of publishing it in this form.

## How it turned out

The start page was posted on 12 May 2024, and the group shared its simplified model as an exported Jupyter notebook on the forum on 17 to 19 November 2024. It is intended both as a backdrop for science fiction and as a possible tool for ex ante evaluation of policies. The target is described in the world as largely achieved, with the work concentrated in low-income and dilapidated buildings. That is fiction, and the policy page says so by omission: it gives no quantified emissions or energy savings, and the simulation behind it assumes identical buildings and is simplified. Nobody has adopted the policy. The residency's stated aim was to test whether science-fictional futures can help evaluate candidate public policies beforehand, and this is the Lab's clearest worked example of that test.
