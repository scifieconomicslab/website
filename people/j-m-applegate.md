J M (Joffa) Applegate co-leads the Complexity Economics Lab at Arizona State University. She sees economies as socially determined arrangements shaped by cultural values and narratives, while holding that general economic principles apply across contexts, and says her career began after she read Kim Stanley Robinson's Red Mars. She has been an Assistant Research Professor at Arizona State University since 2019 and sits on the graduate faculty in Complex Adaptive Systems Science. She trained first as a physicist, with a BS in 1990 and an MS in 1992, then took a PhD in applied mathematics with a complex adaptive systems concentration in 2018 (CV). Her research covers complexity and evolutionary economics, labor, monetary and political economy, and the theory of the firm, and she has published agent-based models such as Emergent Firms and Empathy and Power on CoMSES. The Complexity Economics Lab studies economies as complex adaptive systems, with partners including the Global Climate Forum and the Santa Fe Institute.

## What they bring

Generative agent-based models, economic theory, inroads into adjacent communities from game development to maths.
