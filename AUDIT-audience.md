# Audience audit: prototype-v3

This audit reads the site as each of the six audiences in `brief/audiences.md` would, in the order the brief ranks them: potential partners first (mayors and city officials, then institutions and funders), then economists, writers, practitioners and contributors. It covers the landing page, the method panel and the Our work section, and it records which gaps were filled from sources in this pass and which still need the team.

## The short version

The landing page does its main job. A mayor or programme officer can say what the Lab does within a minute, and the running town gives them something to touch. The weak spot is the step after that, where a sceptical reader asks for evidence. Our work was written as a catalogue of activities, and nearly every entry closed with "the sources do not report results". That reads as a gap even when it is simply honest. The entries that carry the argument (a real place documented as an economy, a plan tested on a model of a real city, a costed policy written before anyone builds it) were also the thinnest.

This pass rewrote the nine entries with the largest evidence gap using primary sources, and corrected two places where the old text did not match them. What is still missing is mostly outcomes that only the team can supply.

## What each audience sees

**Mayors and city officials.** They want to know whether a small team can bring a working model of their town's problem to the next meeting. The hero, the sliders and "Bring us a town" answer that well. In Our work, the entries closest to their situation are the Colombo replica, the Messina cluster and the Great Retrofit policy. Before this pass those three said what the work was but not what a council would get out of it. Each now has the numbers and a short "What to take from it" paragraph: the cost of the Colombo transport plan against national output, who pays and what stops displacement in the retrofit scheme, and what a foundation actually built in Messina. The gap that remains is a case with a named town, a decision and a result. No entry yet shows a place that used the Lab's work to decide something.

**Institutions and funders.** They look for scale, partners and track record. The funder strip and the dated work list help. The weaknesses are that the four offers under Work with us are marked speculative and carry no format, duration, price range or output, and that several entries list "who" as the Lab without saying which partner paid for or hosted the work. The LIRNEasia and SCOPE funding behind the Colombo study, and EIT Climate-KIC behind the Messina papers, are now stated in the entries. A line on the landing page giving the year the Lab started, the number of events and the number of published worlds would help a funder skimming for scale, and every figure for it is already in the notes.

**Economists and complexity researchers.** They want rigour, open code and calibration. The Colombo entry now gives its validation figures against official data (population 1,044,500 against about 1,048,000, road layout over 99 percent) and links the MIT-licensed code. The scifigraph entry gives its counts and the inconsistency the team found in its own extraction. The Great Retrofit entry says plainly that its simulation assumes identical buildings and reports no measured savings, which is the sentence this audience will look for first. The gap is the Linden Cove town model itself, which has a calibration note but no link from Our work, so a researcher cannot go from the work list to the code that drives the hero.

**Writers and worldbuilders.** They want building blocks. The six-plus-one entry was cut off after its first concept and now lists all seven with the novels that use each one. Space Economy Camp now states what failed there (writers detailed governance and spiritual life and skipped central banks) which is the useful lesson for anyone running their own session. Witness and A Town by the Sea are still short on how to play, and there is no obvious "start here" for a writer who wants to use the material.

**Practitioners running local alternatives.** They need to see places like theirs documented. The Messina entry now does that with the cluster's history, its projects and its governance rules, with a warning that the figures are the foundation's own. Costa Rica, Indonesia and South Africa, named in the messaging brief as places the site should treat as viable alternatives, appear nowhere in Our work.

**Contributors and wiki gnomes.** The old section lead, "a note you can read, link to and edit", spoke to this audience on a page aimed at partners. It now reads as an invitation to a visitor, and the edit path remains in the footer. The growth-stage badges (sapling, seedling) on the cards will not mean anything to a mayor. I recommend keeping them on note pages and removing them from landing cards, but have left that as your decision since it touches the garden concept in the brief.

## Still open

These need the team, because no public source supplies them.

- **A decision case.** Nothing in Our work shows a real place using a model or a story to decide something. The brief's own test for the case pages is "a town, a decision and what the model said". If one exists, or is under way in Sicily, it should lead the section.
- **Residency outcomes.** The post-event report, the final participant list and feedback, and the other groups' outputs besides the Great Retrofit. Alberto or Nadia should have them.
- **The School of Future Places and the new alliance in Sicily.** Both entries rest on one line from the home page, and the Lab pages could not be read by the tools used here. Partners, curriculum, dates and audience are all missing, and both are the nearest items to a funder's interest.
- **Work with us offers.** Each needs a format, a duration, what the client brings and what they leave with, and a status that is not "speculative" if the Lab is willing to say so.
- **Entries still thin.** The TEDxBologna talk, the dialogue with Shata Diallo and the 2017 GlocalCamp report have no source links. The Watchdog relaunch, the Witness card game and Witness itself would each gain from one concrete figure.
- **Real names for the Great Retrofit group,** which the start page lists only by forum handle.
- **Verify before publishing.** The webinar dates and speakers should be checked against the Lab's own page, and the figures in the Messina entry against the foundation.
- **Landing proof line.** A single line of counts for scale (see funders above).
- **Links from Our work to the town model's code and calibration note.**

## Second pass: gaps filled by web search

A follow-up search closed several open items.

- **TEDxBologna talk.** Confirmed as 30 June 2022 at the Teatro Manzoni, with the full speaker line-up and a source link. No recording is linked, and the talk title still comes from the brief.
- **Second residency world.** Anissem, a group's 2050 Mediterranean city with characters, an invented governance model and a wiki page, is now in the residency entry. The residency therefore has two documented outputs, not one.
- **Messina recognitions and later figures.** The foundation's own pages list REVES, OECD LEED, FEBEA and the Global Alliance for Banking on Values, which differs from the OECD, UNOPS and WHO named in the 2020 paper. A 2025 interview adds the Premio Innovazione Sociale, more than 260 supported enterprises and about 650 people rehoused. The Messina entry now shows both sets and says which is which.
- **Watchdog.** Rest of World supplies independent figures: a 12-person team, $340,000 from OCCRP, 597 protests and 49 conflicts tracked, and over a million people reached in April 2022. It also contradicts the entry on one point. Rest of World gives about 52,000 users in 90 days in 2019, where the entry said over 200,000. The conflict is flagged in the entry. No source supports the brief's claim that the Ministry of Health used Watchdog software.
- **Still not found.** The Shata Diallo dialogue and the 2017 GlocalCamp report returned nothing usable (a Barcelona event of that year turned out to be a different network's). The School of Future Places and the Sicily alliance exist only on the Lab's own sites, which block automated readers, and the Fondazione's news pages do not mention either. Someone on the team can paste the text of those two pages and I will write them up.

## Method and limits

I read the landing page as rendered, every work note, the offers, the people notes and the brief. For the nine rewritten entries I read the Edgeryders threads, the Fondazione di Comunita Messina announcement, the Watchdog article and the Colombo Skylines project page. The Lab's own website blocks automated readers, so nothing here comes from it directly, and where an entry cited it, the citation is kept as it was. Figures come from those sources as published and were not independently checked. Where two sources disagreed I said so in the entry, and each entry's `confirm` box lists what is left to check.
