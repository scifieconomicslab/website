Two rounds of prototypes have been built. Round one (six pages, in `prototypes/`) tested the flow: a town and a problem go in, a model, a story and a working session come out. Round two (in `prototypes-v2/`) raised the craft. All of it is placeholder: the town model uses invented numbers, the "with you" lines under each block of Lab work (pages 3 to 5) are proposals and the town-and-problem responses on the other pages are templates, forms send nothing, and offers, people and history come from this brief.

### Round two

**1. A town you can run (`p1-town.html`).** A hand-inked isometric solarpunk town made with the Colorfigures skill: homes with solar and grass roofs, a glasshouse, a cistern, a battery, a wind turbine, garden beds and a compost bin, with moving dots for power and water. Sliders and event buttons (drought, storm, newcomers) change sun, rain and households, and the surrounding page redraws live: three coverage gauges for power, water and food, a time chart of the battery, cistern and food stores, and an in and out panel. Below it sit what we do, the town-and-problem form, the four ways to work with us and the people. Palette: warm paper, deep green, sun yellow, sky blue. Type: Fraunces, Figtree and IBM Plex Mono. It best serves the brief's call for lightweight scrollable models paired with text.

**2. The town as a machine (`p2-town-machine.html`).** A 3D town built with the Machine skill, shown on a pale studio floor, with no panels over the picture. As you scroll, the roofs lift off, then the buildings, then the soil, then the underground cistern, battery and digester split away, each stage with a short caption and pinned labels. At the end the layers close again and an amber block marked as the visitor's problem drops into the town, which leads into the "bring us a town and a problem" form. It needs a network connection for three.js and adapts its rendering quality to the machine.

**3. De Stijl (`p3-destijl.html`).** The page is a Mondrian-style grid of thick black rules and primary-colour blocks, set in heavy Jost capitals with Space Mono labels. A pinned section draws the model town in black outline with red, blue, yellow and white faces, then pulls it apart in layers. Visitors pick from coloured tiles, and the real work behind each tile appears beside them with a plan for their own town. Each of the five tiles is tied to work the Lab has already done (Messina residency, The Colombo That Might Have Been, Witness, the General Housing Model, the Worldbuilding Academy and Messina papers); each shows what we did, its status, and what we would do with the visitor as a model, a story and a room. It reads as blunt and structural, closest to an engineering test.

**4. Art Nouveau (`p4-nouveau.html`).** Cream paper, climbing vines at both edges, gold double borders and an arched window, set in Cormorant Garamond italics and Poiret One. The five pill buttons are tied to the Lab's real work, and the hero scene changes with the choice (roof shape, size of the sun); the answer lists what we did and a model, a story and a room offered to the visitor. The town drawing sits inside the arched window in thin green line and soft sage, rose and cream fills. It reads as gentle and organic, and the palette and ornament carry that.

**5. Russian Constructivism (`p5-constructivist.html`).** A poster in red, black and cream with a red wedge, a diagonal black bar and a circle, in Anton capitals and Courier Prime. The visitor picks one of five problems, each tied to work the Lab has done, and the wedge, bar and circle recompose, the slogan changes and four panels show what we did, then model, story and room. The pinned town drawing is rotated and drawn in cream line on black with red and black fills. It reads as urgent and declarative, like a call to build.

**6. Ryoku mode (`p6-ryoku.html`).** After the look of Ryoku Linux: a dark rose theme, kanji section markers and watermarks, numbered labels in the form `00 // LABEL`, Stable and Experimental pills, a dithered picture that changes with the visitor's choices, and a Ctrl K command palette. Four colour palettes (rose, moss, ink, dusk) can be switched from the header. This page was redeveloped around the metaphor of an Obsidian graph, and no longer uses the town. A pinned section draws the lab's own notes as a graph while you scroll: one dot, then the rest arriving one at a time, then the links between them, then size and tint showing which are seedlings, saplings and evergreen, then the notes drifting into clusters (concepts, models, places, essays). Hovering a note shows what it is, and clicking pins it. Filter chips dim everything but one kind of note. At the end the visitor's town and problem become one more note in the graph and draw links to the existing notes closest to them, so the "bring us a town and a problem" step is shown as joining the garden. The links from a town or problem to notes are illustrative. Type: Fraunces, JetBrains Mono and Noto Serif JP. It leans toward the technical and the tinkerer, and is the one prototype that treats the site as software.

**6b. Art Nouveau, garden of notes (`p4b-nouveau-garden.html`).** An alternate to page 4, built on the same graph metaphor with the garden idea taken literally. The notes are buds on a vine: links are curved vine lines, seedlings and evergreens differ in size and in a gold ring, and each kind of note has its own colour (rose, deep green, gold, sage). The graph is drawn inside the arched window as you scroll, and a small version of it fills the arch in the opening screen. Hover and click show a note in an ornate card. As on page 6, the visitor's town and problem join the graph as a new bud. The tone is gentle and organic, and it keeps the vines, double gold borders and Cormorant Garamond italics of page 4.
