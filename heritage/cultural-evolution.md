Cultural evolution is a theory that proposes that humans are subject to evolutionary pressure both at the individual and at the group level. Individual fitness can often be higher in individuals whose behaviour is selfish; group fitness is invariably higher in groups that display collaboration and altruism. These two evolutionary pressures stand in opposition to one another, and together contribute to shaping human behaviour. This theory helps us problematize "human nature", that often economics flattens in unrealistic vision of _homo economicus_. The main proponents of this idea are E. O. Wilson, Robert Boyd and Peter Richerson.
