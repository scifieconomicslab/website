The intellectual heritage is now made of fields, or bright ideas, rather than thinkers. Thinkers are associated to it.

Here's my list. I think the group's list should be compiled additively: if, for example, the simulation dream is important to Yudha it is important to the Lab. In a short time it will become part of the group's intellectual heritage.

* Capability approach: An economy is only as good as the substantive (as opposed to formal) freedoms it provides people who participate in it. Amartya Sen, Giorgos Kallis, Cory Doctorow. 
* Development economics. Economic trajectories are not destiny, they can be influenced by human agency. Albert O. Hirschman, David Graeber, Fabrizio Barca, Ha-Joon Chang, Kim Stanley Robinson. 
*  Economic anthropology. The economy, as perceived by the people participating in it. Essential for literate policy design. Marcel Mauss, James Scott, David Graeber, James Ferguson, Ursula K. Le Guin, Ruthanna Emrys.
* Place-based approaches.  Different places can be impacted by the same dynamics in different ways. This gives rise to spatial inequalities and threatens cohesion and solidarity. Fabrizio Barca, Jason Hickel, David Graeber, Thomas Piketty.
* Econophysics. The economy is a material phenomenon. Always look for the hidden costs and human suffering associated. Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen, Gerry McGovern. 
* Network science. Used to think rigorously about social and economic relationships and the patterns they form. Georg Simmel, Jacob Moreno, Albert-László Barabási, Duncan Watts. 
* Radical uncertainty. Tools to deal with uncomputable, incompressible uncertainty beyond risk. Friedrich Hayek, Nassim Taleb, Steve Keen.
* Engineering approaches. Used to test economic theories and policy options against real data. François Quesnay, Augusto Graziani, Julia Steinberger, Joel Millward-Hopkins.
* Evolutionary approaches and path-dependency. Understand economic phenomena in non-reversible time, where the past influences and constrains (but does not fully determine) the future. Joseph Schumpeter, Jane Jacobs, Albert O. Hirschman, Stuart Kauffman, Peter Watts, Ada Palmer. 
* Simulation modelling. A way to think about  emergence, by setting up a model, tweaking its parameters and observing where the interactions between the agents therein lead it. Brian Arthur, Peter Turchin, Leigh Tesfatsion, Tynan Sylverster, Charles Stross.
